# 03 - Short-form craft: platform guidance, specs, retention, anti-slop

Research date: 2026-10-26. Scope: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts, Facebook Reels for Skanyx's 20-40 s vertical scenario videos.
Legend: **[DATA]** = platform-published figure or study; **[OFFICIAL]** = platform guidance/policy text; **[OPINION]** = practitioner/agency view; **[TEMPLATE]** = widely used community template, not platform-published.

Status: complete (2026-10-26). Sections 1-8 answer the research questions; "Decisions for the studio" and "Uncertain / verify" follow; Appendix A is the raw source log with every URL.

Ground rule for reading this file: platform-published numbers are mostly about **ads**, from the platform's own (self-interested) analysis, often 2021-2024. They are the best evidence available, not controlled science. Where a number is a vendor's or a guru's, it says so.

## 1. Official platform creative guidance

### TikTok

- **[OFFICIAL] "Don't make ads, make TikToks"** (TikTok for Business blog, 2021-09-06). The slogan is an invitation, not a rule: fit the platform's style, make for TikTok instead of resizing, "less polished, but real life and authentic content works best" (advertiser quote), shoot vertical and plan transitions, involve customers, show how the product works instead of elaborate visuals, keep production cost low; ads must still be labelled. https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/what-we-mean-when-we-say-dont-make-ads
- **[OFFICIAL] Creative best practices for performance ads** (Ads Help, last updated June 2025):
  - "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds"; "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds for better recall and awareness".
  - "Use captions or text overlays to provide context"; "We recommend displaying 5-10 words per second when using text" (see section 3: this conflicts with reading-speed research, treat with care).
  - Favour a DIY, less polished look; feature creators, employees or customers; use sound/music; 9:16 at 720p+; keep key content in the UI safe zone.
  - Structure: hook, then unique selling points, then a clear CTA. Test creatives that differ a lot (concept, not colour); refresh when delivery decays.
  - https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices
- **[OFFICIAL + DATA] TikTok Creative Codes** (TikTok for Business page, undated, (c) 2026; figures from TikTok/Kantar research 2021-2022): six codes = TikTok-first, Trends, Production, Structure, Stimulation, Sound.
  - "90% of ad recall impact is captured within the first six seconds".
  - Product on screen: +65% brand affinity, +25% recall. CTA card at the close (not interrupting the content): +45% recall, +19% likability.
  - "Faster scene changes tend to draw viewers in early"; text overlays raise view time, recall and likability.
  - "88% of TikTok users say sound is vital" (Kantar survey, US, Jan 2021: a survey answer, not measured sound-on share).
  - https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads
- **[DATA] "Triple-digit conversion lift"** (TikTok blog, 2021-12-21; TikTok's automated scan of its own ad network): 21-34 s ads +280% conversion vs outside that window; 720p+ +312%; true 9:16 without black bars +91%; text CTA +152%; a variety of scenes +38% vs one continuous shot; captions/on-screen text showing the offer +80%; human voiceover plus written offer +87% (explicitly NOT validated in Europe or Brazil); on-screen text in the first 7 s +43% (gaming only). https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-that-drives-conversions
- **[DATA] Vidmob x TikTok** (1,678 ads, 7.3 B impressions, Jan-Oct 2023; reported by Adweek 2024-04-02): everyday people beat celebrity talent 1.7x; direct-to-camera talking head +14% 2-second view-through and +50% "hooking power"; a product shot on its own +17% 6 s view-through; making the brand the hero of the story 1.5x hooking power; **opening on a logo only: -14% 6 s view-through**. https://www.adweek.com/adweek-wire/report-reveals-key-visual-and-creative-ad-strategies-for-hooking-tiktok-audiences/
- **[DATA] Creator-led vs brand ads** (TikTok analysis Feb 2024-Jan 2025, reported 2025-12-03): creator-led ads +70% CTR at equal CPM, +159% engagement; Kantar: viewers skip influencer ads after 17.8 s on average vs 7.9 s for traditional branded content. https://www.netinfluencer.com/tiktok-report-shows-creator-led-ads-drive-70-percent-higher-click-through-rates/

### YouTube Shorts

- **[OFFICIAL] YouTube publishes almost no creative rules for organic Shorts.** The creator page (https://www.youtube.com/creators/shorts/) is a tooling tour. The creative guidance lives in Google's ads material:
- **[OFFICIAL] Google "ABCDs for Shorts"** (blog.google, 2024-04-17): top Shorts ads "feel like they're part of a viewer's Shorts feed", resemble creator-made content, are casual ("everyday people speak directly to the viewer") and upbeat; Google's general ABCD = Attract (hook from the first frame), Brand (early, naturally), Connect (emotion, story), Direct (clear CTA). https://blog.google/products/ads-commerce/youtube-shorts-ads-select-lineups-abcds/
- **[OFFICIAL + DATA] Google Ads vertical video help**: keep logo, product and supers inside the red safe area of the 1080x1920 template; vertical performs better than landscape on Shorts (Google data 2024: adding vertical lifted Shorts VTR >40%, watch time +20%); label AI-created or AI-edited assets where law requires (EU named). https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9128498
- **[OFFICIAL] The hook metric YouTube exposes**: "Viewed (vs. swiped away): the percentage of times viewers viewed your Shorts versus swiped away" (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281). The often-quoted "aim for 75-80% viewed" is vendor opinion.
- **[OFFICIAL] View counting changed 2025-03-31**: a public Shorts view now counts on every start or replay, no minimum watch time; the old metric lives on as "engaged views" in Advanced mode. Judge Shorts on engaged views, viewed-vs-swiped and average percentage viewed, never on public views. https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/

### Meta (Instagram Reels + Facebook Reels)

- **[OFFICIAL + DATA] Meta developer blog "Unlock the power of Reel ads"** (2024-11-07): build 9:16 **with audio**, key messages inside the safe zone, hook at the start, CTA at the end; ">75% of Instagram Reels views are sound-on"; campaigns following Meta's Reels "creative essentials" saw 2x higher delivery to Reels (Meta data, Mar-May 2024). https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/11/07/unlock-the-power-of-reel-ads/
- **[OFFICIAL] Meta for Creators "Combating unoriginal content"** (2025-07-14), five practices: post content you filmed or made yourself (gets the most distribution; you may share the same content across your own Pages/profiles); make meaningful enhancements ("simply stitching together clips or adding your watermark does not qualify"); **"Tell a story ... avoid very short videos that give viewers little value"**; no visible third-party watermarks or visibly recycled content; captions that are relevant, with no links, minimal capitalization and at most five hashtags. https://creators.facebook.com/blog/combating-unoriginal-content
- **[OFFICIAL] Instagram ranking signals** (Mosseri video, Jan 2025): "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends"; sends weigh more for reaching non-followers, likes for followers. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/
- **[OFFICIAL] Instagram originality** (April 2026 extension, reported by PetaPixel 2026-04-30): accounts that repeatedly (10+ times in 30 days) post others' content without material enhancement are not recommended to non-followers; reposts get replaced by the original in recommendations. https://petapixel.com/2026/04/30/new-instagram-policies-target-reposted-content/
- **[OFFICIAL] Trial Reels** (Instagram, Dec 2024): a professional account can show a Reel to non-followers first, read the result (~24 h, up to 72 h window) and only then share it to followers. Eligibility thresholds come from secondary sources (200+ followers for professional accounts). https://routenote.com/blog/how-to-test-reels-with-non-followers-on-instagram/

### Synthesis: what all four platforms agree on

1. Native, creator-like, casual beats polished-commercial (TikTok, Google, Meta all say it in their own words).
2. The opening frame carries the hook; brand/logo openers cost viewers (Vidmob -14%); the value proposition belongs in the first 3 s, the hook is complete within 6 s.
3. Sound on by design (TikTok 88% "vital", Meta >75% of IG Reels views sound-on), with text on screen so it still works muted.
4. Human presence (everyday people, direct address, real hands) lifts hooking power; product shown on screen lifts recall.
5. Originality is now enforced in distribution on every platform (sections 4 and 8).

### Covers / first frame

- Instagram profile grid crops to 3:4 (center 1080x1440, i.e. y 240-1680) since 2025-01-19. TikTok's grid is also a portrait crop **[OPINION/secondary]**. A cover whose title sits inside the union rectangle survives every crop.
- YouTube Shorts: mobile can only pick a frame (plus text/filters); uploading a custom image is YPP + desktop only (2026). So **design a real frame of the video as the cover**, not a separate poster.
- The first frame is also the frame people see before playback; it must already show the hook (no fade from black, no logo).

## 3. Retention craft

### Hook (first 1-3 s)

Evidence:
- **[DATA, TikTok]** 90% of ad recall impact within the first 6 s; value proposition in the first 3 s improves recall; face/person in the first 2 s raises hooking power ~50% and ad recognition 32% (TikTok material via The Drum); talking head +14% 2 s view-through; logo-only opening -14% 6 s view-through (Vidmob).
- **[DATA, TikTok/CreatorIQ]** creators saying "you" in the first five seconds associated with +128% purchase intent. https://www.creatoriq.com/press/releases/tiktok-creatoriq-release-special-report-with-data-backed-keys-to-success-for-advertisers
- **[DATA, Meta, old]** people recall mobile feed content after 0.25 s of exposure (Fors Marsh for Facebook, 2016); Facebook/Nielsen 2016: brand lift appears even in views under 3 s. https://martech.org/even-brief-video-views-drive-brand-lift-facebook-nielsen-study-finds/
- **[OFFICIAL, YouTube]** "Viewed vs. swiped away" is the per-Short hook score.

Hook formulas that fit Skanyx's scenario format **[OPINION, practitioner consensus, mapped to the data above]**:
1. **The moment itself** (visual-first): a real dashboard, the light blinking on, the real chime/ding, the driver's real reaction. No words needed for the first second. Matches "brand as hero of the story" and "product/problem on screen".
2. **Direct address of the viewer's situation**: "If this light just came on, here's whether you can keep driving." (uses "you", states the value in < 3 s).
3. **The stakes question**: "Safe to drive, or call a tow truck?" with the severity colour already visible.
4. **The cost shock**: "This light cost one driver 1,800 EUR. It didn't have to." (only with a sourced number from REPAIR_PRICES).
5. **The myth**: "Everyone says a flashing check-engine light is fine for a few days. It isn't." (myth-busting is a format the mechanic accounts use; must be fact-checked).
6. **The POV**: hands plugging the adapter under the dash, first-person (ChrisFix-style POV is the dominant format of the biggest repair channel).
Rotate archetypes; never reuse one opening sentence verbatim across videos (see section 8).

### Pacing and pattern interrupts

- **[DATA, TikTok]** "faster scene changes tend to draw viewers in early"; a variety of scenes +38% vs one continuous shot; 5+ scenes +171% (gaming ads).
- **[OPINION]** Common practice is a new visual every 1.5-3 s and a "pattern interrupt" (cut to a new angle, a punch-in on the app screen, a sound cue, a text change) every 3-5 s. There is no platform-published optimum shot length; the evidence only says "more scenes, faster early".
- Meta's own warning cuts the other way: "avoid very short videos that give viewers little value" and "tell a story". So pace serves the story; it does not replace it.

### Length

- **[DATA, TikTok 2021]** 21-34 s ads converted best (+280%).
- **[DATA, vendor]** Socialinsider, 6 M Reels H1 2026: 45-60 s had the best engagement rate (0.35%) and median views; under 30 s "respectable but outpaced" (vendor benchmark, reported secondhand). https://www.shortimize.com/blog/video-length-sweet-spots-tiktok-reels-shorts
- **[OPINION]** For Shorts, 15-45 s for a single idea is the most consistent claim; no YouTube-published length data exists.
- Conclusion: 20-40 s is well-supported for a one-problem story; let the story set the length (a 24 s video with no padding beats a 40 s one with a recap).

### Looping

- **[OFFICIAL]** YouTube counts every replay as a public view since 2025-03-31; TikTok's newsroom (2020) says strong signals such as finishing a video weigh more than weak ones.
- **[OPINION]** End on a frame/line that flows back into the opening (e.g. the last line answers, the cut returns to the dashboard). Do not fake a loop with a jarring jump; a seamless loop only helps if the content is worth a second watch.

### Sound-off, voice vs text

- **[DATA]** Instagram: >75% of Reels views sound-on (Meta 2024). TikTok: 88% "say sound is vital" (survey). Facebook feed history (2016) was mostly muted; Verizon/Publicis 2019 survey (n=5,616 US): 69% watch with sound off in public, 80% more likely to finish a video with captions (self-reported). https://www.3playmedia.com/blog/verizon-media-and-publicis-media-find-viewers-want-captions/
- Implication: design **sound-on first** (voice carries the story), but every beat must also read with sound off (burned-in captions plus the key facts as on-screen text: the code, the severity colour, the cost range).
- **[DATA, TikTok 2021]** human voiceover + written offer +87% conversion, but not validated in Europe. Treat as weak evidence for VO, strong evidence for "text + voice together".

### Caption styling and readability

Evidence base:
- **[OFFICIAL]** Netflix English timed text guide (change log 2025-12-19): max 42 characters per line, max 2 lines, max 20 characters per second (adults), break lines after punctuation and before conjunctions/prepositions, never split article+noun or first+last name, bottom-heavy "pyramid" shape. https://partnerhelp.netflixstudios.com/hc/en-us/articles/217350977-English-USA-Timed-Text-Style-Guide
- **[OFFICIAL]** BBC subtitle guidelines: 160-180 words per minute (secondary sources; page not fetchable from here).
- **[OFFICIAL, TikTok]** "5-10 words per second when using text". This is 2-4x the Netflix reading ceiling (20 cps is ~3.5-4 English words/s). Read it as "keep text moving and short", not as a reading speed.
- **[OPINION]** Word-by-word "Hormozi" captions (bouncing coloured words, emoji, pops) are now so widely cloned that editors describe them as template fatigue; no controlled study either way. https://joyspace.ai/hormozi-editing-style-2026-analysis

Derived rules (see Decisions):
- Phrase-based captions, 1-2 lines, <= ~26-32 characters per line at our type size, synced to VO phrases, at most ~15-17 characters per second (Netflix adult ceiling 20 cps, lower because a phone feed is a glance, not a sit-down read).
- Type size: on a 390-pt-wide iPhone a 1080 px frame displays at ~0.36x, so Apple's 17 pt body size equals ~47 px on the canvas; captions should be ~56-72 px (cap-height ~40-50 px), hook text ~80-110 px. (Derived from Apple HIG body size 17 pt; https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)
- Contrast: WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3, 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large; over moving footage use a solid or 70-85% opaque plate or a strong shadow, never thin outline text. https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#contrast-minimum
- Position: inside the union safe rectangle; captions in its lower third (y ~1000-1240), never under the platform caption/username block (y > 1248).

### Search (on-screen text and speech are indexed)

- **[OPINION, multiple SEO sources; TikTok has not documented it]** TikTok and Instagram read captions, on-screen text (OCR) and transcribed speech to match search queries. TikTok search volume for "car repairs" in the UK alone is ~365,000/month (Auto Trader, 2025-03). Saying and showing the code ("P0420", "check engine light", the make) in the first seconds, in caption and on screen, costs nothing and is how a panicked owner finds the video. https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/03/13/uk-study-finds-vehicle-repair-and-cleaning-tiktoks-are-misleading/

## 4. "AI slop" in 2025-2026: what reads as AI or low effort, and what platforms do about it

### What audiences notice (evidence)

- **[DATA] NielsenIQ** (2024; >2,000 viewers, ~150 with EEG): consumers "can readily identify AI-generated video ads", rate them more "annoying", "boring" and "confusing", with weaker memory activation than traditional ads. https://getcoai.com/news/nielsen-consumers-find-ai-generated-advertising-annoying-boring-and-confusing
- **[DATA] Raptive** (2025-07-15, n=3,000 US): content merely *suspected* of being AI cut trust by ~50%, even when humans wrote it; ads next to it were rated 17% less premium, 14% less relatable, 11% less trustworthy. https://ppc.land/raptive-study-shows-ai-content-cuts-reader-trust-by-half/
- **[DATA, preliminary academic]** TikTok panel of 21,541 videos from 3,647 creators: AI-generated voice associated with ~5-7% fewer likes, comments and shares (working paper, core.ac.uk works/153500852; not peer-reviewed in the version found).
- **[DATA] Kapwing "AI Slop Report"** (late 2025): of the first 500 Shorts served to a fresh account, 21% were AI-generated and 33% "brainrot". The feed is saturated, so anything that pattern-matches to it gets swiped. https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/
- **[DATA] Auto Trader UK** (2025-03, 130 car TikToks): about two-thirds misleading. In our niche the "slop" is also *inaccurate* advice; being verifiably right is a differentiator, not just hygiene.

### The tells (practitioner lists; [OPINION] but consistent across sources)

Voice: one prosodic level throughout ("TTS plateau"), no breaths, no micro-hesitation, no emphasis, mispronounced codes/brands, the same stock voice heard on thousands of videos. Script: chatbot phrasing, generic claims, list-shaped ("3 reasons why..."), the hook formulas everyone uses. Visuals: generic stock footage loosely related to the narration, AI imagery (hands, gibberish text on signs, over-smooth camera, over-saturated colour, identical lighting in every shot). Edit: the same caption template (bouncing coloured words, emoji), over-animated text, every cut on the beat. Structure: every video the same hook -> list -> CTA, which viewers learn and skip. Sources: https://www.opus.pro/blog/ai-slop-aesthetic-12-tells (2026-04-17), https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10475903/v1 (Korean "voice slop" = AI script + TTS + stock + minimal captions).

### Platform statements and mechanics that touch reach

- **YouTube**: YPP "inauthentic content" (renamed from "repetitious", effective 2025-07-15) bars content that "looks like it's made with a template", "templated storylines", slideshows or scrolling text "with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value", videos "interchangeable from video to video", and "AI-generated content from generic templates" without the creator's own insight (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392). This is a monetisation policy, and Skanyx will likely not monetise, but Neal Mohan's 2026 letter (2026-01-21) commits YouTube to "reducing the spread of low-quality, repetitive content" with its anti-spam/clickbait systems, which is a distribution statement. https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/21/neal-mohans-2026-letter-to-creators-a-lot-of-ai-a-tiktok-shop-competitor-and-beautifully-produced-tv-to-compete-with-netflix/
- **Meta**: reduced distribution on *everything* an account posts if it repeatedly posts unoriginal content; duplicate copies lose distribution; original, story-led video gets the most (2025-07-14). Instagram originality: see section 1.
- **TikTok**: "Content is also ineligible for the FYF if it includes unoriginal or reused material without anything new" (Community Guidelines effective 2026-09-24). Since 2025-11 users have an **AI-content slider** ("see less AI-generated content") and TikTok reads invisible watermarks / C2PA Content Credentials to identify AIGC. Content detected as AI can therefore be filtered out by the very viewers who dislike it. https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity ; https://www.techspot.com/news/110315-sick-ai-slop-feed-tiktok-finally-you-turn.html (2025-11-19)

### Disclosure rules that collide with "nobody should suspect AI" (important for the voice decision)

- **Meta**: users must label posts with "photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered"; secondary readings of Instagram's help page list "a reel narrated with a realistic AI-generated voiceover" as an example that needs the label; Meta auto-applies "AI info" when it detects industry signals (C2PA/IPTC). Penalties for not labelling. https://www.androidauthority.com/meta-ai-labels-3411262/ ; https://minter.io/blog/everything-you-need-to-know-about-ai-labels-on-instagram/
- **TikTok**: creators must label AIGC "that contains realistic images, audio or video"; whether a synthetic narrator voice over real footage counts is not spelled out.
- **YouTube** (answer/14328491, fetched 2026-10-26): no disclosure needed for "cloning your own voice for voiceovers or dubs", AI-assisted scripts, captions, audio repair; disclosure needed for realistic content that makes a real person appear to say or do something. YouTube auto-labels content carrying C2PA metadata or detected internally, and a C2PA-triggered label cannot be removed. Non-music AI-audio labels show in the expanded description.
- **EU AI Act Art. 50** applies from 2026-08-02 (deepfake disclosure for deployers; machine-readable marking duty for providers of generative systems, with a reported grace period to 2026-12-02 for systems already on the market). Whether a synthetic narrator voice of no real person is a "deep fake" is a legal question for the owner, not this file.
- **Net effect**: a stock synthetic voice is the single most likely thing to get a Skanyx video *labelled* as AI on Meta (by rule) and possibly on TikTok/YouTube (by detection), which defeats the owner's bar no matter how good the rest is. The only paths that satisfy both the bar and the rules: a **real human voice** (team member or a hired native speaker), or the team member's **own** voice clone where the platform exempts it (YouTube does explicitly; Meta's rule on realistic synthetic audio does not obviously exempt it). This file does not recommend hiding AI use; it recommends not needing to.

### Counter-practices (what to do instead)

1. **Real people, real cars, real places.** Team-filmed B-roll on the team's own cars (two BMWs per project memory) and friends' cars; real dashboards with the real light; real garage or roadside. No stock clips, no AI imagery, no AI-generated B-roll (Meta: original footage gets the most distribution; YouTube/TikTok: unoriginal = ineligible).
2. **A human voice with a human cadence**: breaths, emphasis, a smile on the good news, a pause before the cost. Record room tone; do not de-breath. If synthesis is used anywhere, it is the team member's own consented voice, and the per-platform disclosure check is done first.
3. **Specifics a template can't produce**: make, model, mileage, the actual code, the real app result on that car, a sourced cost range with currency, the thing the owner was about to do (drive to work, sell the car). Pull numbers from `data/dtc-stats.json` and `REPAIR_PRICES.md`, never invent.
4. **Real app, real data**: screen recordings of the actual app scanning the actual car (or a faithful replay of a real scan); no mock UI that the app cannot produce (claim safety: `CAPABILITY_GROUND_TRUTH.md`).
5. **Restrained typography and motion**: one type family, phrase captions, no emoji rain, no bouncing words, motion only where it explains (a severity bar filling, the cost range appearing). The brand's design standard (Emil Kowalski skills) already says "should it animate at all?" first.
6. **Imperfection that is real, not faked**: handheld micro-shake on B-roll, ambient sound (indicator ticking, engine idle, door chime), natural light. Not a "vintage filter" (a filter is itself a tell).
7. **Varied hooks and structures** across the library (section 8); no recurring catchphrase except an intentional, human sign-off.
8. **Accuracy as a visible value**: say "roughly", show the range, name the uncertainty ("could be the sensor or the cat; the app tells you which is more likely" only if the app actually says that). In a niche where 2/3 of videos mislead, being careful reads as human expertise.
9. **No other platform's watermark, no recycled exports**: render a clean master per platform from Remotion.
10. **Community signals of a real account**: reply to comments with video replies, show the team, admit mistakes. AI farms do not do this.

### What this means for Skanyx's format

The scenario story already has the winning shape (problem -> product solves it on screen -> plain-language verdict). What lifts it from "app ad" to "premium" is: (a) a recognisable human (a team member as a recurring presence, or at least the same real voice and the same real hands), (b) the car and the moment being real, (c) one specific, sourced, surprising fact per video, (d) restraint in edit and type. Faceless is viable only if (b)-(d) are excellent; the data says a human face or voice adds measurable hook power.

## 6. Music for a business account

- **TikTok [OFFICIAL]**: business accounts see only the **Commercial Music Library** (~1 M pre-cleared tracks); "Businesses should instead use the Commercial Music Library for all commercial TikTok activities", including organic posts, duets/stitches, ads and branded content (Ads Help, last updated July 2025). Using the general library from a personal account to promote a business risks infringement. CML licences are TikTok-only (secondary). https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/commercial-music-library
- **Instagram/Facebook [OFFICIAL wording via secondary]**: the licensed library is "intended for personal, non-commercial use"; certain business accounts and post types have no access; the **Meta Sound Collection** (~9-14 k tracks, sources differ) is royalty-free and cleared for FB/IG only. Boosted/paid posts need independently licensed music. https://www.foximusic.com/blog/instagram-reels-music-copyright-legal-guide/ (vendor, updated 2026-10-02)
- **YouTube [OFFICIAL]**: Shorts audio library songs carry an invisible Content ID claim (fine, most usable up to 90 s); YouTube Audio Library (royalty-free) gets no claim; music added outside the Shorts tools is subject to normal claims, and **a Short over 1 min with any active Content ID claim is blocked globally**. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13486873
- **Trending sounds**: a brand account effectively cannot use chart/trending songs on TikTok or IG; on YouTube it can via the Shorts library but loses nothing by not doing so. "Platform availability does not equal licensing permission" (vendor example of a football club whose business-account reel hit 7 M views and then faced sync-rights demands).
- **What it means for Skanyx**: voice-led stories do not need trending music. Use (1) a small set of **owned or cross-platform-licensed beds** (a subscription library whose licence explicitly covers business use on all four platforms, with the YouTube channel whitelisted against Content ID; verify the licence text), kept low under the VO, rotated; (2) **real diegetic sound** (dashboard chime, indicator, engine, adapter click) as the signature, which no one else owns; (3) TikTok CML only for TikTok-only edits. Our own audio can become a reusable TikTok "original sound" if it is distinctive.

## 7. Multi-language strategy

### Platform features

- **YouTube multi-language audio [OFFICIAL]**: upload your own dubbed tracks "to a single video or Short"; requires Advanced features; the track defaults to the viewer's preferred language (inferred from watch history). https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784
- **YouTube auto-dubbing [secondary, 2026-05-15]**: 27 languages, 8 with "expressive speech" (EN, ES, PT, FR, DE, IT, HI, ID); dubbed versions average ~75% of the original's view duration; lip-sync is a pilot; creators can review/unpublish dubs. Polish/Lithuanian not confirmed; Shorts eligibility not confirmed. AI dubs are labelled. https://ppc.land/youtube-auto-dubbing-tops-6m-daily-viewers-expands-to-27-languages
- **Meta AI translations for Reels [OFFICIAL]**: launched 2025-10-09; on Instagram now EN/ES/HI/PT + five Indian languages + FR/DE/IT/JA/KO (2026-07-14); Facebook adding AR/ID/FR/TH/VI (announced 2026-06-04). AI mimics the creator's voice, optional lip-sync, labelled **"Translated with Meta AI"**; viewers can switch it off. https://about.fb.com/news/2025/10/discover-reels-around-world-meta-ai-translation/
- **TikTok**: caption/description/sticker translation for viewers in a limited language set; AI dubbing (Symphony) is an ads-side tool. No creator-side multi-audio for organic posts found.
- **Recommendation signals [OFFICIAL, TikTok 2020]**: language and country settings get *lower weight* than interactions, but they are signals; a feed of mixed languages on one account sends mixed signals to followers and to the system.

### Tension with the format

Burned-in captions and on-screen text are language-specific. A YouTube multi-audio Short with English burned-in text would show English text over German audio; Meta's auto-translation keeps the original on-screen text and adds a visible AI label. So **auto-translation and multi-audio do not fit a captioned, text-carrying format** unless the on-screen text is language-neutral (codes like P0420, the severity colour, a EUR figure) and captions are delivered as platform subtitle tracks.

### What EU app brands do

- Duolingo: global English account + separate accounts for big non-English languages with local hires; English markets served by the global account. AUTODOC and MS Motorservice publish per-language video channels for repair content. HubSpot recruits separate German and French YouTube hosts. (Trade press / job listings; [OPINION-grade evidence].)
- Agency consensus [OPINION]: one account per language once that language's audience and output justify it; one shared account with bilingual captions before that.

### Recommendation

Render **every video natively per language** (own VO by a native human speaker, own captions, own on-screen text, localised cost currency/format per `data/term-banks/`), then publish:
- **One English global account per platform** (covers UK/IE + English-speaking viewers everywhere).
- **Separate local-language accounts** only for the markets with the most traffic and a native voice available (candidates from the site's own data: DE, PL, ES, LT; re-check against install data). Start them when there is a sustainable cadence (a dead account is worse than none).
- Do **not** post 14 languages into one account; do **not** rely on Meta/YouTube AI dubbing for primary markets (AI label + voice mismatch), though leaving Meta's translation available for non-target languages is a low-cost option the owner can decide on.
- YouTube: either per-language channels, or one channel with multi-audio *only* for a text-neutral variant of the template with subtitle tracks per language. Per-language channels are simpler and match how AUTODOC runs repair content.

## 8. Repetition risk: one template, many fault codes

### How platforms treat near-identical videos

- **YouTube [OFFICIAL]**: "Inauthentic content" = looks templated, "interchangeable from video to video", "templated storylines", minimal variation; **allowed**: the same intro/outro if "the bulk of your content is different", and a series with a recurring format if "each video has a different storyline, focus, or concept". Plus the 2026 commitment to reduce the *spread* of low-quality repetitive content.
- **TikTok [OFFICIAL]**: unoriginal/reused material "without anything new" is FYF-ineligible; repeated FYF-ineligible posts can make the whole account temporarily ineligible (TikTok trust & safety statement, secondary); the FYF "generally won't show two videos in a row made with the same sound or by the same creator" and excludes duplicated content (newsroom 2020).
- **Meta [OFFICIAL]**: duplicate videos get reduced distribution; repeat unoriginal posting reduces distribution on the whole account; story-led original video is favoured. Meta's policy targets reposting *others'* work, but its duplicate detection is automatic, and a library of 200 near-identical renders is exactly what it is built to catch.
- No platform publishes a numeric similarity threshold. Any "X% must differ" is our own rule.

### How much variation (derived rules, [OPINION] grounded in the policy wording)

The policies punish *interchangeability* and *lack of narrative/insight*, not a recurring series format. So the template may fix the **grammar** (pace, type, colour system, end card, voice) but every video must change the **content** that a viewer would notice:
1. **Different real footage**: a different car, dashboard and location for each video; no B-roll shot reused within ~10 videos on the same account; the opening shot is always unique.
2. **A different story**: who, where, what they were about to do, what happened. The scenario is the content, the code is the topic.
3. **A different script**: written per video, with code-specific facts (meaning, safe-to-drive verdict, cost range, the most common cause on that make from `dtc-stats.json`); no sentence other than the sign-off appears in more than a few videos (the site already enforces this idea for CTAs: preflight CHECK 13).
4. **Rotating hook archetypes** (6 listed in section 3) and at least **3 structural variants** (e.g. chronological scenario; verdict-first then why; myth -> correction; POV tutorial; "what the app showed vs what the garage quoted").
5. **Rotating music beds**, and real diegetic sound that is naturally different per car.
6. **Mixed formats on each account**: template videos are at most about half the feed; the rest are human-led (team talking to camera, comment replies, used-car check walk-throughs, myth-busting, behind the scenes). This keeps the account from reading as a render farm in the eyes of both viewers and the "repeated FYF-ineligible content" account-level rule.
7. **Prioritise codes by demand** (the site's GSC/Ads data and the app's own scan stats) rather than rendering the whole library: 30 excellent videos beat 287 interchangeable ones.

### B. Style-guide rules (hook, pacing, captions, voice)

1. **Frame 0 shows the problem**, real: the light on a real dashboard, the chime audible. The viewer knows the topic before any word lands.
2. **The spoken hook is complete by 3.0 s** and uses "you" or the viewer's exact situation; the value promise (meaning / safe? / cost) is stated or implied in it.
3. **The code and plain-language topic appear in the first 3 s** in voice, on-screen text and the post caption (search).
4. **The app is on screen by ~5-8 s**, doing the real scan on the real car (product as hero, shown not described).
5. **Three answers, three beats**: what it means; can I drive (severity colour); roughly what it costs (sourced range, local currency). Each beat is its own visual.
6. **New visual every 1.5-3 s; nothing static longer than 3 s** except the verdict hold (<= 2.5 s). Pattern interrupt (angle change, punch-in, sound cue) every 3-5 s. Pace serves the story; no cut without a reason.
7. **Every key fact is both heard and read**; the video must work muted and must also work eyes-off (voice alone).
8. **Human voice, human presence**: a real recorded voice (native speaker per language); real hands, a real car, ideally a recurring real team member. No stock voice, no stock footage, no AI imagery.
9. **One template grammar, never one script**: rotate the six hook archetypes and the structural variants; no sentence except the sign-off may repeat across more than a few videos; never a list-shaped "3 reasons why" by default.
10. **Restraint in motion and type**: no bouncing word-by-word captions, no emoji, no sound-effect on every cut; animate only what explains (severity bar, cost range).
11. **Loop by design**: the last line/shot resolves and leads back to the opening frame.
12. **Accuracy is the brand**: every claim checked against `CAPABILITY_GROUND_TRUTH.md`, every price against `REPAIR_PRICES.md`, every code against `data/dtc-codes/`; say "roughly" and show ranges.
13. **Music is a bed, not the hook**: low under VO, rotated, licensed for business use on all four platforms (TikTok-only edits may use CML); diegetic car sound is the signature.
14. **Post copy**: plain sentence with the code and symptom, no links, sentence case, <= 5 hashtags (Meta guidance), no "link in bio" spam.
15. **Measure the hook, not the views**: YouTube "Viewed vs swiped away" + engaged views; Instagram Trial Reels to test hooks on non-followers before sharing; TikTok 2 s/6 s retention in analytics.

### C. Account and distribution decisions

- Business/professional accounts on all four platforms (needed for analytics, ads, Trial Reels); accept that TikTok then restricts music to the CML.
- One English global account per platform + local-language accounts only for the top non-English markets with a native voice and a sustainable cadence (candidates DE, PL, ES, LT; confirm with install data). Never mix languages on one account.
- Template videos at most ~half of each account's output; the rest human-led formats (team to camera, comment replies, used-car check walk-throughs, myth-busting).
- No reliance on Meta/YouTube AI dubbing or translation for primary markets (AI labels, voice mismatch, burned-in text stays in the source language).

### D. Per-video quality checklist (all must pass before publish)

1. [ ] Frame 0 shows the real problem (no logo, no black, no fade) and works as the cover in the 3:4 crop.
2. [ ] Spoken hook ends by 3.0 s; the code/topic is on screen and spoken in the first 3 s.
3. [ ] The real app is visible doing a real scan by ~8 s; every screen shown is something the app actually produces.
4. [ ] What it means, can I drive (severity colour), rough cost: all three answered, each as its own beat.
5. [ ] Every number traced to a source file (REPAIR_PRICES, dtc-stats, dtc-codes); currency and number format correct for the locale.
6. [ ] No capability claim beyond `CAPABILITY_GROUND_TRUTH.md`; store wording "free on iOS and Android".
7. [ ] All text, logos and faces' eyes/mouths inside the union safe rectangle (x 120-888, y 288-1248); TikTok right rail clear; checked with the overlay toggle on.
8. [ ] Captions: phrase-based, <= 2 lines, <= 28 chars/line (32 for long-word locales), <= 17 cps, >= 56 px, contrast >= 4.5:1, Netflix break rules, no orphan word.
9. [ ] No visual held > 3 s (verdict <= 2.5 s); a scene change at least every 3 s; total length 20-40 s with no padding or recap.
10. [ ] Voice is a real human recording (or a disclosed, platform-checked own-voice clone): natural breaths and emphasis, correct pronunciation of the code, make and model.
11. [ ] All footage team-filmed for this video or not reused within the last ~10 videos on the account; no stock, no AI imagery, no gibberish text in frame.
12. [ ] Music licensed for business use on the target platform (TikTok: CML or owned; IG/FB: Sound Collection or licensed; YouTube: Audio Library or licensed and whitelisted); bed sits clearly below VO.
13. [ ] Script read aloud by a second person: no chatbot phrasing, no stock hook formula used in the last ~10 videos, no sentence copied from another video except the sign-off.
14. [ ] Muted test (watch with sound off: story still clear) and eyes-off test (listen only: story still clear).
15. [ ] Native-speaker review for every non-EN render (vocabulary from `data/term-banks/<loc>.json`), including on-screen text and captions.
16. [ ] Loop check: the last 2 s lead naturally back into frame 0; end card inside the safe rectangle.
17. [ ] Export check: 1080x1920, CFR 30 (or 25) fps, H.264 High yuv420p, AAC 48 kHz, no watermark, correct language master.
18. [ ] Device check on one iPhone and one tall Android in the real app UI (draft/private upload or Trial Reel): nothing important under the UI or cropped at the sides.
19. [ ] Post copy: code + symptom in a plain sentence, no links, <= 5 hashtags, correct AI-disclosure setting per platform rules.
20. [ ] Variation check against the last 10 posts on the account: different car, location, hook archetype, structure variant and music bed.

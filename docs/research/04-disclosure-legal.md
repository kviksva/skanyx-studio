# 04 - Disclosure, labelling and legal: AI voice, demo data, trademarks

Research for the Skanyx short-form video studio. Researched 2026-10-26. **Not legal advice.** It summarises platform rules, EU texts and official guidance with sources and dates, and flags what needs an owner or lawyer decision. Every claim carries its source; "verify" marks anything I could not confirm against a primary source on the day.

Status: COMPLETE (2026-10-26). Sections 1-7 answer the seven research questions; section 8 is the decision table; 9 owner decisions; 10 lawyer questions; 11 uncertain/verify.

## Bottom line

- **Always required, regardless of AI:** TikTok "Disclose commercial content > Your brand" on every promo post (TikTok Community Guidelines, effective 2026-09-24). Apple and Google badge/device rules (one static black App Store badge placed first, Google Play badge the same size or larger, no animated/tilted iPhone, no iPhone next to an Android phone, credit lines, "Sequences shortened" on sped-up app footage). Truthful demo: everything shown must exist in the app; implied "this reading came from this car" must be true or carry a "Demo data" note.
- **The AI narrator voice is the main element that triggers AI disclosure** (AI music is the other one, on YouTube, which lists "AI generated music" as needing disclosure). Meta requires a label for "realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created" (no own-voice carve-out). YouTube explicitly exempts cloning one's own voice for voiceovers/dubs, and is silent on a stock voice. TikTok's current rule names "realistic-looking scenes or people", which leaves audio unclear. The EU AI Act (Art 50(4), applicable since 2026-08-02) may treat a voice designed to pass as human as a "deep fake"; the Commission Guidelines (2026-07-20) do not settle it, and they treat a company's own CEO avatar as a deep fake even with consent. Lawyer question.
- **Not AI-disclosure triggers:** real B-roll, the real app screen recording, Remotion motion graphics, AI-drafted scripts and captions (YouTube calls these "production assistance"), and minor audio clean-up.
- **ElevenLabs output is watermarked** (inaudible, SynthID-based, rolled out to all tiers from mid-2026) and anyone with a free ElevenLabs account can check it with the Audio Detector, so "nobody can tell" does not hold against anyone who looks. ElevenLabs' own Prohibited Use Policy (2026-10-09) requires disclosure "where required by law or where failing to do so would materially mislead", and forbids stripping provenance to disguise AI content.
- **Biggest contract surprise:** Eleven Music's Free to Pro plans are "For Individual Use Only"; a company needs the Scale plan or higher. Professional Voice Clones can only be made of your own voice, on your own account.
- **Cleanest way to meet the owner's goal:** a human voiceover, at least in the main languages. With an AI voice, the minimum is Meta's label, and probably TikTok's and YouTube's too, plus a lawyer's view on the EU AI Act.

## 1. TikTok

### 1a. AI-generated content (AIGC) labelling

**The rule (primary source).** TikTok Community Guidelines, "Integrity and Authenticity" > "Edited Media and AI-Generated Content (AIGC)" (released 2026-08-25, effective 2026-09-24): "we require creators to label AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic-looking scenes or people." Unlabelled content "may be removed, restricted, or labeled by our team, depending on the harm it could cause." Even labelled, TikTok does not allow content "misleading about matters of public importance or harmful to individuals."
Source: https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity (fetched 2026-10-26).

What the current text does and does not say:
- The trigger is **realistic-looking scenes or people**. The current Guidelines section gives no examples and does not mention voice or audio at all. It does not define "significantly edited".
- Earlier TikTok wording (2023-2024, as reported by NBC/AP) was "AI-generated content that contains realistic images, audio or video". So **realistic synthetic audio was explicitly in scope in the earlier wording**; the 2026 text is shorter but not narrower in intent. Source: https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/national-international/tiktok-to-start-labeling-ai-generated-content-as-technology-becomes-more-universal/5399109 (2024-05-09). TikTok's own support page on AIGC (https://www.tiktok.com/support/faq_detail?id=7636670084747893268) is JavaScript-rendered and could not be read on 2026-10-26: **verify its current examples in the app/browser**.
- Applied to Skanyx: real B-roll of real cars is not AIGC. A Remotion motion-graphics layer is not a "realistic-looking scene or person". A screen recording of the real app is not AIGC. The **open question is the AI narrator voice**: a realistic human-sounding synthetic voice over real footage is the one element that arguably falls under "AI-generated ... realistic" (audio). TikTok's text does not settle it either way.

**Auto-labelling via C2PA Content Credentials.** Since 2024-05-09 TikTok reads C2PA Content Credentials on uploads and "can use [them] to instantly recognize and label AIGC"; images and video first, "coming to audio-only content soon". Source: https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/partnering-with-our-industry-to-advance-ai-transparency-and-literacy (2024-05-09). In Nov 2025 TikTok added a user slider to see less AI content in For You and began testing an invisible watermark applied to its own AI tools' output and to uploads that carry C2PA credentials (secondary coverage: https://techcrunch.com/?p=3068861 ; https://www.unite.ai/tiktok-introduces-user-controls-for-ai-generated-content-in-feeds/ , Nov 2025; official newsroom post not located, **verify**).
- Practical consequence: if a final MP4 exported from our pipeline carries a C2PA manifest that declares generative-AI ingredients, TikTok may auto-label the whole video. A Remotion/ffmpeg render does not write C2PA by default; whether an ElevenLabs audio track's provenance survives being muxed into a video is covered in section 4e.
- Note on reach: the Nov 2025 AI-content slider means an "AI-generated" label can plausibly reduce distribution to users who turned AI content down. That is a commercial reason the owner cares, **not** a reason to avoid a required label (removal/restriction is the penalty for not labelling).

**Penalties.** Removal, restriction (reduced distribution/For You ineligibility) or TikTok applying the label itself, scaled to harm (Guidelines, above).

### 1b. Commercial content disclosure (business account promoting its own app)

- TikTok Community Guidelines, "Regulated Commercial Activities" (released 2026-08-25, effective 2026-09-24): "If you're posting commercial content on TikTok, you must clearly disclose it using the content disclosure setting." This covers promoting **your own** business, product or service, not only paid third-party endorsements. Undisclosed commercial content: TikTok may "reduce its visibility or apply the content disclosure setting"; repeat failures can lead to posting restrictions or a ban. Source: https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/regulated-commercial-activities (fetched 2026-10-26).
- How: post screen > "Content disclosure and ads" > turn on "Disclose commercial content" > choose **"Your brand"** vs "Branded content" (label "Paid partnership", for a third party). TikTok's help page says the setting adds a "Commercial content" label; third-party guides say "Your brand" shows "Promotional content" (the in-app wording may vary by region/version, **check in-app**). The label cannot be changed after posting; to fix it, delete and repost. Source: TikTok Ads Help, "How to turn on the Commercial Content Disclosure setting in TikTok" (last updated January 2026): https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-turn-on-the-commercial-content-disclosure-setting-in-tiktok?lang=en ; label names corroborated by https://support.vistasocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/41057119178523-Promotional-Content-Disclosure-on-TIkTok (third party, verify in-app).
- **So every Skanyx brand-account video that promotes the app should be posted with "Disclose commercial content" > "Your brand".** This is TikTok policy, not just good practice; EU consumer law independently requires commercial intent to be identifiable (UCPD Annex I point 11, Art 7(2); section 5).
- **Music on a TikTok Business Account:** the in-app sound picker shows only the Commercial Music Library (CML), pre-cleared for commercial use; uploading a video with independently licensed music (e.g. our own AI-generated track with a commercial licence) is allowed as long as the licence covers commercial use on TikTok. Source: TikTok for Business blog, https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/audio-library-royalty-free-music (verify date). Do not use trending/General-library sounds from a personal account to promote the app.

## 2. YouTube (incl. Shorts)

### 2a. Altered or synthetic content disclosure

Primary source: YouTube Help, "Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content", https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en (no publication date on page, "(c)2026 Google"; fetched 2026-10-26).

**Core rule:** "We require creators to disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic." Disclosure is required when GenAI content:
- "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do."
- "Alters footage of a real event or place."
- "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur."
- "Creates music that's the main focus of the video."
The list is stated to be non-exhaustive. Minor edits are those "primarily aesthetic" that do not "alter the content in a way that could mislead the viewer".

**Official examples that DO need disclosure:** AI-generated music; AI-generated extra footage of a real place; realistic AI video of real athletes; making it appear someone gave advice they did not give; a realistic tornado moving toward a real city; fake hospital scenes; a public figure stealing; a real person arrested.

**Official examples that do NOT need disclosure** (verbatim list items, the ones relevant to us in bold):
- Not realistic: unicorn fantasy world; green screen floating in space; AI missile animation in a fully animated video.
- Minor: beauty filters; colour/lighting filters; special-effects filters (background blur, vintage); **"Production assistance, such as using generative AI to create or improve a script, outline, thumbnail, title, or infographic"**; **caption creation**; video sharpening/upscaling/repair and **voice or audio repair**; idea generation; **"Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs"**; gameplay footage; **"AI generating or extending a backdrop to simulate a moving car"**; **"Using effects to enhance previously recorded audio"**.

What this means for Skanyx (my reading, not YouTube's words):
- **Own-voice PVC** (a team member cloning their own voice, in any language) is an explicit YouTube "no disclosure needed" example ("voice overs or dubs").
- **Stock/designed ElevenLabs voice narrating real footage** is not in either list. It does not make a real person say anything, does not alter a real event, and does not create a realistic scene. On the criteria it does not require disclosure, but the page also says labels "may appear in the expanded description for non-music AI-generated audio", i.e. YouTube expects some non-music AI audio to be disclosed. Grey zone: **owner decision / verify**.
- **Motion graphics (Remotion)** are not GenAI and not realistic: no disclosure.
- **AI script help** (Claude drafting the script): explicit "no disclosure" (production assistance).
- **AI music**: "AI generated music" is on the requires-disclosure list, but the criterion text is "music that's the main focus of the video". A quiet AI bed under narration is arguably not the main focus; a video built around an AI track is. Simplest safe path: use licensed library music, or tick "AI use" when AI music is audible. **Owner decision.**
- **Demo data screen recording**: not GenAI, so not this policy (see section 5 for consumer-law angle).

**How to disclose:** "AI use" (altered content) setting in the upload flow / Studio "Attributes". Label placement: a label in the video player "may appear" for AI music or photorealistic AI content; otherwise in the expanded description (non-music AI audio, non-photorealistic/animated AI content).

**Automatic labels:** YouTube "may automatically apply a label" for content made with YouTube's GenAI tools, **content containing C2PA metadata**, or content its internal systems detect as AI-generated. The creator gets a Studio notification and in most cases can switch it off by answering "No", **except** labels from YouTube's own AI tools, **C2PA metadata**, or manual review, which cannot be changed. So a C2PA manifest declaring AI that survives into our upload would produce a label we cannot remove.

**Consequences:** Disclosure "won't limit a video's audience or affect monetization eligibility". Creators who "consistently" fail to disclose may get a label applied, content removal, or YPP suspension.

### 2b. Paid promotion disclosure

YouTube Help, "Add paid product placements, sponsorships & endorsements", https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235?hl=en (fetched 2026-10-26; only date on page is 2025-10-10, about the EU political-ads change). The checkbox is "My video contains paid promotion like a product placement, sponsorship, or endorsement"; it adds a disclosure label at the start of the video. Paid product placements are defined as content "created for a third party in exchange for compensation".
- The page does **not** address a brand promoting its own products on its own channel. On the definition (third party + compensation), a Skanyx video on the Skanyx channel is not "paid promotion". Ticking it is harmless but not required by YouTube's wording. **Verify** (no explicit official statement found).
- EU consumer law still requires the commercial intent to be clear from context (section 5). On a channel named Skanyx showing the Skanyx app with a download CTA, the commercial nature is evident; the risk is low but a lawyer can confirm.
- If a third-party creator is ever paid or given free Pro to post about Skanyx, **they** must tick paid promotion (and on TikTok use "Branded content").

### 2c. Inauthentic ("mass-produced") content: YouTube Partner Program

YouTube Help, "YouTube channel monetization policies", https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en. Changelog 2025-07-15: "repetitious content" renamed **"inauthentic content"**, to "better clarify this includes content that is repetitive or mass-produced". Not monetisable: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates" that appears mass-produced; repeated same-situation/same-outcome videos. Allowed: identical intros/outros if most content differs; series where each video has a "distinct storyline, focus, or concept"; using AI to "edit your video scripts or generate a unique background visual" where the final product shows creative vision. Separately, channels using **AI personas presenting as human experts on health, legal, financial or political topics** are not monetisable.
- Relevance: this is a **monetisation** policy (YPP), not a distribution ban. A brand channel that does not earn ad revenue is not directly affected. But it signals how YouTube's systems classify templated AI videos; a Remotion pipeline that re-skins one template across 14 languages and many DTC codes looks exactly like "templated storylines" unless each video carries distinct content (real footage of a real car, a specific code, a specific explanation). Treat it as a quality bar, not a legal duty.
- The AI-voice question is not addressed in the policy text.

## 3. Meta (Instagram Reels, Facebook Reels)

Meta's help-centre and Transparency Center pages are JavaScript-rendered and refused automated reads on 2026-10-26, so the primary sources below are Meta's newsroom posts. **Verify the current help-centre text in a browser** ("Label AI content on Instagram", https://help.instagram.com/761121959519495).

**Organic-post disclosure duty (the strictest of the three platforms for audio).** Meta newsroom, "Labeling AI-Generated Images on Facebook, Instagram and Threads" (2024-02-06, page also shows an update dated 2025-04-01): Meta requires people to "use this disclosure and label tool when they post organic content with a photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created or altered", and "may apply penalties if they fail to do so". Source: https://about.fb.com/news/2024/02/labeling-ai-generated-images-on-facebook-instagram-and-threads/
- Third-party guides (2026) quote the help-centre wording as content with "photorealistic video or realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created, modified or altered, including with AI"; still images are not in the mandatory scope; the toggle is "Add AI label" in the Reel/post composer (Stories: Options > Label AI). Sources: https://www.postora.org/blog/ai-generated-content-labels-social-media-2026 ; https://www.sirency.com/blog/instagram-ai-label-rules (third party, 2026, verify).
- **Applied to Skanyx:** a realistic synthetic narrator voice is, on its face, "realistic-sounding audio that was digitally created". Meta's text has **no** carve-out for a voiceover over real footage, and no YouTube-style carve-out for cloning your own voice. On a plain reading, **every Reel with an ElevenLabs voice (stock or own-voice PVC) falls within Meta's self-disclosure duty.** This is the single clearest platform-side disclosure trigger in the whole study. (Contrast: a human team member reading the script into a microphone triggers nothing.)

**How labels appear.** "Made with AI" was renamed "AI info" on 2024-07-01 after retouched photos were mislabelled; from 2024-09-12, content "only modified or edited by AI tools" has the label moved into the post's menu, while content detected as **generated** by AI keeps a visible "AI info" label. Meta may add a more prominent label where content poses "a particularly high risk of materially deceiving the public on a matter of importance". Source: https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/ (2024-04-05, updates 2024-07-01 and 2024-09-12).

**Automatic labels from C2PA / IPTC.** Meta reads the "AI generated" information in the **C2PA and IPTC** metadata standards and invisible markers. As of the Feb 2024 post, Meta said it "can't yet detect those signals and label [audio and video] content from other companies" and relied on self-disclosure for audio and video (same Feb 2024 source). Whether Meta now auto-labels video from C2PA in 2026 was not confirmed from a primary source (**verify**). Practical rule: if our export carries no C2PA/IPTC AI assertion, auto-labelling depends on Meta's own classifiers only.

**Profile label (separate rule).** Third-party reports say Instagram added an "AI creator" profile label in May 2026, renamed "AI-generated profile" on 2026-08-31 and scoped to accounts that "feature an AI-generated person". Not applicable to a brand account that shows real people and real cars. Source: https://www.techwyse.com/?p=78112 (third party, verify).

**Commercial disclosure on Meta.** The "Paid partnership" label (Branded Content tool) is for a creator promoting a business partner; a brand's own Page/account posting about its own product does not use it. Meta's branded content policies govern third-party creators. (Not separately researched in depth; standard practice, **verify** if a creator programme starts.) Ads (paid boosts) are a separate regime: from June 2026 AI labels on ads sit in a unified "About this ad" menu (https://socialmediatoday.com/news/meta-adds-updated-disclosure-tags-for-ai-generated-ads/824658, secondary). Boosting a Reel turns it into an ad and pulls in Meta's ad policies and the EU political/DSA ad rules.

**Who we are under the Act.** MB "Skanyx" using ElevenLabs for its marketing videos is a **deployer** (a legal person using an AI system under its authority for professional purposes; Guidelines para 12-14). Staff and freelancers working under its control are not separate deployers. ElevenLabs is the **provider**. A company that merely commissions an agency and does not control its AI use is not a deployer (Guidelines p.7), which does not apply here because we produce in-house. Lithuanian market-surveillance authority for the AI Act: **Ryšių reguliavimo tarnyba (RRT)** (Law XV-106 of 2025-01-14 per secondary sources; Commission list: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/market-surveillance-authorities-under-ai-act , **verify**).

**Penalties** (Guidelines para 152, citing Art 99): up to EUR 15 000 000 or 3% of worldwide turnover, whichever is higher; **for SMEs and start-ups, whichever is lower**; fines must be proportionate and take SME viability into account.

### 4b. The two deployer duties in Art 50(4)

1. **Deep fakes** (image, audio or video): deployers "shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated". For content forming part of an "evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work or programme", the duty is limited to disclosure "in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work."
2. **AI-generated or manipulated text published "with the purpose of informing the public on matters of public interest"**: must be disclosed, **unless** it "has undergone a process of human review or editorial control and where a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility".
Art 50(5): clear and distinguishable, "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure", accessible. (Art 50 text as quoted in the Code of Practice, p.29.)

**The human-review exception applies to TEXT only, never to audio/video deep fakes.** Reviewing a voiceover script does not exempt a deep-fake voice.

### 4c. Is our narrator voice a "deep fake"? (the crux)

Art 3(60): "AI-generated or manipulated image, audio or video content that resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful". The Guidelines (paras 113-116, pp.33-36) read this as four cumulative criteria:
- **Resemblance** must be "appreciable" (high similarity; case-by-case, objective comparison).
- **Existing** is read broadly: "it is sufficient for simulated persons ... to resemble someone or something that exists, **can plausibly exist** or could have plausibly existed". Only the clearly unrealistic (dragons, flying humans) is out.
- **Persons** expressly include "personal characteristics or expressions, such as image, **voice**, behaviour, performances".
- **Falsely appears authentic or truthful**: authenticity "refers to whether the content is genuinely what it purports to be in terms of its source or creation process (including e.g. **the involvement of real human beings** ...)". Assessed as a whole: resemblance, message, deployment context, the environment, the foreseeable audience **and its expectations**; no intent to deceive is needed; diverse audiences (elderly, low AI literacy) count (para 115). Photorealism is "not determinative".
- Minor AI edits are out (colour correction, noise reduction, "adapting audio parameters", background extensions in product ads) (para 116).

Official examples (Guidelines p.36), the ones nearest to us:
- **Deep fake:** "AI-generated audio involving voice cloning of a newspaper podcast's regular presenters"; "AI-generated video featuring a realistic synthetic avatar of a company CEO congratulating employees" (note: a company's own CEO, presumably consenting, is still a deep fake); "AI-generated video featuring an AI-generated depiction of a celebrity influencer in an advertising or promotional context"; an AI image that makes a product look better than reality.
- **Not a deep fake:** "AI voice replication for fictional characters (e.g., for audiobooks, games or animation) **when there is no deception as to the identity of the narrators**"; a radio broadcast with AI audio levelling/noise reduction that does not alter the words; a real product (e.g. a car) shown in an ad against an AI-generated background, so long as the product is not misrepresented; a presenter walking a real landscape with an AI animation illustrating data.

My reading for each Skanyx element (**not legal advice; the classification is the deployer's own case-by-case assessment and a lawyer should confirm**):
- **Real B-roll, real app screen recording, Remotion motion graphics:** not AI-generated, so outside Art 50 entirely.
- **Stock or designed ElevenLabs narrator voice (resembles no one in particular):** genuinely borderline. For: "persons" includes voice; "can plausibly exist" covers an invented but realistic human; authenticity includes "the involvement of real human beings", and the owner's goal is exactly that viewers believe a human recorded it. Against: no identifiable person is resembled, the voice is a disembodied narrator (no claim about who is speaking), and the Guidelines' "fictional characters ... no deception as to the identity of the narrators" example points the other way. **Not settled by the Guidelines. Lawyer question #1.** A conservative reading treats a voice designed to pass as human, in an informative/commercial video, as in scope.
- **Own-voice PVC of a team member:** closer to the Guidelines' deep-fake examples (cloned real presenters; the company CEO's synthetic avatar). If the video presents or implies that this person is speaking (face on camera, name, "I"), it very likely is a deep fake **even with full consent**: consent goes to personality/data-protection rights (paras 127-129), not to the labelling duty. If the voice is an anonymous narrator, it reduces to the stock-voice question above. **Lawyer question #2.** Contrast YouTube, which explicitly exempts cloning one's own voice; the EU test is different.
- **AI-generated music/SFX:** the Guidelines list "AI-generated music in any kind of genre resembling the individual style of existing artists" as a deep fake under the lighter artistic regime (p.39). Generic background music that resembles no artist arguably resembles no "existing person, object, place, entity or event" and so is out of scope. Low risk; a credit line ("Music: AI-generated") in the caption would satisfy even the lighter regime.
- **The "evidently artistic/creative" lighter regime does not help us.** It is "interpreted strictly"; it "excludes content ... if its nature is exclusively informative or commercial"; where content is both, "the informative character should always prevail"; ads qualify only "in certain, specific situations"; every advertising example in the Guidelines that is a deep fake gets standard labelling (teleshopping-style simulated consumers; a "realistic synthetic influencer testing out a sponsored real product") (paras 122-124, p.39). A car-diagnostics explainer is informative and commercial.
- **AI-drafted script / on-screen text:** Art 50(4) text duty covers text published to inform on matters of public interest. The Guidelines list as OUT of scope "AI-manipulated text that is part of a company's advertisement or product descriptions (**not including any claims related to e.g. health, consumer safety or sustainability**)" (p.42), and list "consumer safety" and "public health" among public-interest topics. So a video caption or on-screen text claiming whether a fault is "safe to drive" is arguably consumer-safety text. The human-review exception covers it if (i) a person with relevant knowledge substantively reviews and fact-checks it (spell-checks and cursory approval do not count; any AI change after sign-off voids it) and (ii) a natural or legal person holds editorial responsibility, with identity and contact details publicly findable (paras 133-138). Skanyx already has `/editorial-policy`; the video workflow should log a named human reviewer per script.

### 4d. How to label if something is in scope (Code of Practice, Section 2, Commitment 1, pp.29-33)

- **Visual:** an icon or label whose main element is the capitalised acronym **"AI"** (EU icon set optional: "Basic", "Fully AI-Generated", "Partially AI-Modified"; the icon page's own example of a custom label is a deepfake video labelled **"voices generated with [AI icon]"**). Embedded in the content (or an equivalent platform overlay), clear at first exposure, not covered by overlays, "e.g., in the top right corner of an image or video deep fake", and **at the beginning of the video and, where possible, at regular intervals**; displaying it throughout the deep-fake part is encouraged.
- **Audio deep fakes:** a "short audible disclaimer in plain and simple natural language, either in the same language as the content or in English" at the beginning; when a screen is available (a phone is) an additional visual icon. For a **visual** deep fake an audible disclosure is only additional, never a replacement.
- **Platform tools count, with limits:** where a VLOP (TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, Facebook are all designated VLOPs) provides a labelling tool that gives a clear and distinguishable disclosure, "those deployers can rely on such tools to fulfil their transparency obligation ... within the context of the VLOP" (Guidelines para 126). But disclosure must not be "hidden under layers of menu options" (para 142): Meta's menu-only "AI info" for edited content and YouTube's expanded-description label are weaker than an on-video mark. **A burned-in "AI voice" mark is the safe route if the voice is judged in scope.**
- **Machine-readable marks do not count for deployers:** "deployers cannot rely on the machine-readable marking embedded in the content by the provider under Article 50(2)" (Guidelines para 117).
- **No retroactivity:** content generated before 2026-08-02 need not be labelled (Guidelines para 153-154; Reed Smith 2026-07-28).

### 4e. Provider-side marking: ElevenLabs

- Art 50(2): providers must mark outputs "in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated". The Guidelines list "Synthesis of realistic speech in a specific person's voice" among semantic changes that require marking (p.29); the Code (Section 1, Measure 1.1, p.8-9) asks providers for "at least two layers of machine-readable marking": digitally signed metadata recording whether content is AI-generated, plus an imperceptible watermark; and (Measure 1.2(b), p.10) to put in their terms "a prohibition of the intentional removal of or tampering with metadata markings by deployers", with an exception for good-faith processing where transforming metadata is necessary downstream. So a provider that signs the Code will, sooner or later, ship signed metadata on audio too, and its terms will forbid us from deliberately stripping it. Re-encoding audio into a video in the normal course of editing is arguably the "good faith, legitimate processing" exception; deliberately scrubbing marks to hide AI use is not. AI Omnibus: systems on the market before 2026-08-02 have until **2026-12-02** for Art 50(2) (Guidelines para 153).
- ElevenLabs (official docs, fetched 2026-10-26): "ElevenLabs embeds watermarks into audio we generate"; the watermark is inaudible, placed at masked frequencies; a free **Audio Detector** (signed-in users) checks it, falling back to the older AI Speech Classifier; third-party models offered through ElevenLabs are generally not watermarked. Source: https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/audio-tools/audio-detector and https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/legal/audio-detector/what-is-watermarking-and-why-is-eleven-labs-using-it
- The technology is Google DeepMind's **SynthID**, announced around 2026-06-25, rolled out first to free-tier TTS and "throughout July 2026" to all tiers and to Music, Sound Effects and Dubbing; audio from before June 2026 is unmarked. Sources: https://www.createwith.com/tool/elevenlabs/updates/elevenlabs-embeds-synthid-watermarks-in-ai-audio-launches-free-detector-tool (2026-06-25, secondary); ElevenLabs help article "Is my audio being watermarked right now?" https://help.elevenlabs.io/hc/en-us/articles/48099714323857 (403 to automated fetch; quoted via search snippet, **verify**).
- **Does it trigger platform auto-labels?** ElevenLabs, official FAQ: "No other platforms can currently detect ElevenLabs watermarks ... [it] is not connected to other platforms' monetization or content policies." https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/legal/audio-detector/will-watermarks-cause-my-content-to-be-flagged-downranked-or-demonetized-on-you-tube-or-other-platforms . ## 5. Showing the app with demo/fixture data; claims in ads

### 5a. The legal frame (EU consumer law)

The Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC (UCPD, as amended by the Omnibus Directive 2019/2161) applies to a trader's own organic social posts: they are "commercial practices". Text: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj/eng (wording below checked against the retained copy at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2005/29/chapter/2/section/1 and https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eudr/2005/29/annex/I, fetched 2026-10-26). Commission UCPD Guidance: Notice 2021/C 526/01 (2021-12-29), https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:52021XC1229(05). Lithuania implements it in the Law on the Prohibition of Unfair Business-to-Consumer Commercial Practices, enforced by VVTAT (State Consumer Rights Protection Authority) (**verify** current LT citation).
- **Art 6(1)(b), misleading action:** false or deceptive information about "the main characteristics of the product, such as its ... benefits, risks, ... fitness for purpose, usage, ... specification ... or the results to be expected from its use, or the results and material features of tests or checks carried out on the product", where it is likely to change a transactional decision (here: downloading the app or buying Pro/PPI).
- **Art 7(2), misleading omission:** includes failing "to identify the commercial intent of the commercial practice if not already apparent from the context". On a channel named Skanyx showing the Skanyx app, commercial intent is apparent; TikTok's "Your brand" toggle (section 1b) closes the gap anyway.
- **Annex I point 11 (always unfair):** paid editorial content not disclosed as such ("advertorial"). Relevant only if we pay a creator or publication.
- **Annex I point 12 (always unfair):** "Making a materially inaccurate claim concerning the nature and extent of the risk to the personal security of the consumer or his family if the consumer does not purchase the product." This is the provision a fear-led hook ("this light could kill you, download now") runs into if the risk is overstated.
- **Annex I point 20 (always unfair):** describing a product as "free" "if the consumer has to pay anything other than the unavoidable cost of responding". The app is free to download, but reading a car needs an ELM327 adapter (bought separately) and some features are Pro/PPI. "Free app" is a standard app-store description, yet a video that says "free car diagnostics" while the reading shown requires an adapter and a Pro feature invites a complaint. Say what is free.
- **Art 12, substantiation:** authorities can require the trader "to furnish evidence as to the accuracy of factual claims" and treat claims as inaccurate if the evidence is missing or insufficient.
- Penalties since the Omnibus Directive: for widespread infringements handled through coordinated CPC action, the **maximum** fine Member States provide must be at least 4% of the trader's annual turnover in the Member States concerned, or at least EUR 2 million where turnover data are unavailable (Art 13(3)-(4) UCPD as amended by Directive 2019/2161). The realistic first step for a small trader is an order to stop or amend.
- **Coming:** the Digital Fairness Act proposal is expected around 2026-11-11 (secondary, https://www.privacylaws.com/news/eu-proposal-on-digital-fairness-act-expected-by-the-end-of-2026/ ; earlier Commission statements said "by end of 2026"); influencer marketing is in scope; unlikely to apply before 2028. **Watch.**

### 5b. Demonstrating an app with simulated data: what regulators have said

There is no EU text specifically on "demo data in app ads". The closest settled practice is the UK ASA's line of rulings on mobile-game ads, which applies the same misleadingness test as the UCPD (the CAP Code is UK self-regulation, not binding in the EU, but EU self-regulatory bodies in EASA reason the same way):
- Footage must be **representative of what the user actually gets**. Simulated or non-typical footage is acceptable only if it is not materially different from the real experience, and a clear on-screen statement where footage is simulated helps; a disclaimer cannot rescue a materially misleading depiction. Rulings: AppQuantum "Gold and Goblins" (https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/whats-a-gold-to-a-goblin/), Playrix "Homescapes/Gardenscapes" 2020 (https://www.rpclegal.com/snapshots/advertising-and-marketing/playrix-gameplay-footage-must-be-representative-of-the-gaming-experience/), King of Avalon 2017 (https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2017/asa-rulings-on-mobile-games-developers). Secondary summaries; **read the ASA adjudications before relying on exact wording.**
- CAP on AI in ads (CAP News, 2025-05-29): "no blanket legal requirement in the UK to disclose the use of AI in ads"; ask (1) "Is the audience likely to be misled if the use of AI is not disclosed?" and (2) does the disclosure clarify or contradict the message; an AI image showing a product effect that does not reflect real-world results is misleading **even with** an AI disclosure. https://www.asa.org.uk/news/disclosure-of-ai-in-advertising-striking-the-balance-between-creativity-and-responsibility.html
- EASA (European Advertising Standards Alliance) press release 2026-07-30: welcomes the Commission Guidelines and Code, "regretting a tendency in the Guidelines to extend the concept [of deep fake] beyond the wording of the AI Act", and says it is "developing advertising-specific guidance that prevents consumer deception without causing disclosure fatigue". Not published yet; **watch** for it. https://www.easa-alliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/EASA-Press-Release-Gen-AI-1.pdf
- UK Advertising Association, "Best Practice Guide for the Responsible Use of Generative AI in Advertising" (Feb 2026, voluntary, risk-based): https://adassoc.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Best-Practice-Guide-for-the-Responsible-Use-of-Generative-AI-in-Advertising.pdf (not read in full).

**Applied to Skanyx (my reading):**
- A real screen recording of the real app is a fair demonstration **of the interface**. Fixture data becomes a problem only through the **implied claim** the edit makes. Cutting from B-roll of the team's BMW (plugging in the adapter) to the app showing a fault code and a verdict implies "the app read this from this car". If the fixture shows a code, values or a verdict that this car did not produce, or a result the app cannot produce for a real car, that is the Art 6(1)(b) risk.
- **The app ships its own Demo Mode** ("three pre-built scenarios (healthy, minor issues, serious faults); explore the whole app with no adapter and no signup", `content/blog/_SYSTEM/CAPABILITY_GROUND_TRUTH.md` line 22). Recording Demo Mode is recording the real product; the honest on-screen wording is then simply "Demo mode". If the studio's Simulator fixtures differ from the shipped Demo Mode scenarios, they are no longer "the app as a user sees it" and need the care below.
- Three clean options, in order of preference: (1) **fixtures built from a real capture** of the car on screen (record a real session on the E60/E61, replay it in the Simulator), so the demo is true even though it is replayed; (2) where the screen and the car are not linked, edit so no link is implied; (3) where a link is implied and the data is invented, a short on-screen **"Demo data"** / **"Simulated screen"** note at the moment the screen appears. The note is cheap; it does not cure a capability the app does not have.
- Every capability shown or said must exist in the shipping app for the platform and car type shown. The repo's claim authority is `content/blog/_SYSTEM/CAPABILITY_GROUND_TRUTH.md` (e.g. Skanyx does NOT produce a probability-ranked list of causes; Fault Prediction is questionnaire-based; PPI is ICE petrol US 1996+/EU 2001+ and diesel EU 2004+; Wi-Fi adapters unsupported per MEMORY). Script review against that file should be a standing gate in the studio pipeline.
- Apple's App Store rule that app previews may only use captures of the app applies to the App Store product page, not to social ads (App Store Review Guideline 2.3.4, https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ , **verify** current numbering). Still a good discipline.

### 5c. "Safe to drive" and repair-cost figures

- **"Safe to drive" / urgency verdicts** are risk-to-personal-safety claims. Overstating danger to drive downloads touches Annex I point 12; understating it ("it's fine, keep driving") is an Art 6(1)(b) "risks" claim and, beyond advertising, a safety and liability question. The app's verdict is generic per code and severity, not an inspection of this car. Wording that stays defensible: "what the code usually means and how urgent it tends to be", "the app's verdict for this code", plus "check with a mechanic if ... ". Never present a verdict on a filmed car as a safety certification. It is also "consumer safety" content for AI Act text purposes (section 4c): keep a named human reviewer on every script.
- **Repair-cost figures** are price-related claims about third-party services, not about our own price, so the Price Indication Directive does not bite, but Art 6(1) still applies to the "results to be expected". Use ranges, say "typical EU range" and the source basis, and keep them identical to the site's canonical ranges (`content/blog/_SYSTEM/REPAIR_PRICES.md`; a $ or GBP figure is never relabelled as EUR). A single precise number on screen ("this will cost EUR 412") reads as a quote for that car.
- **Our own prices** shown in a video (Pro EUR 79.99/yr etc.) must be the current price for the market shown; say "final price" not "VAT included" (CLAUDE.md, MB is not VAT-registered). A struck-through "was" price triggers the Omnibus prior-price rule (Price Indication Directive 98/6/EC Art 6a): avoid in organic video.
- **Comparisons with competitors** (Carly, OBDeleven, BlueDriver) are comparative advertising under Directive 2006/114/EC: verifiable, like-for-like, no denigration, and checked against `content/blog/_SYSTEM/COMPETITOR_PRICES.md`.

## 6. Trademarks and IP: BMW badges, car makes, Apple and Google imagery and badges

### 6a. BMW badges on our own car; naming car makes

- **Law:** EU trade mark law lets a third party use a mark "for the purpose of identifying or referring to goods or services as those of the proprietor", in particular to indicate the intended purpose of a product or service, provided the use is "in accordance with honest practices in industrial or commercial matters" (Directive (EU) 2015/2436 Art 14(1)(c); EUTMR 2017/1001 Art 14(1)(c); https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2015/2436/oj/eng). Leading case: **BMW v Deenik, C-63/97 (1999-02-23)**: BMW could not stop an independent garage using the BMW mark to tell the public it repairs and maintains BMW cars, unless the use creates the impression of a commercial connection (e.g. that it is an authorised dealer). CJEU press release: https://curia.europa.eu/en/actu/communiques/cp99/cp9907en.htm . The honest-practices test (Gillette C-228/03) adds: no impression of a trade connection, no taking unfair advantage of the mark's reputation, no discrediting it (**verify citation details**).
- UK *BMW v Technosport* [2016] EWHC 797 (IPEC) drew a line between the word mark (fine to say you work on BMWs) and the **roundel/M logos**, which the average consumer associates with authorised partners (secondary: https://trademarkblog.kluweriplaw.com/?p=550 ; UK law, persuasive only).
- **Applied to Skanyx (my reading):** filming the team's own E60/E61 with the badge visible, as the real car the app is plugged into, is incidental and truthful. Risk rises when the roundel becomes the **hero of the frame or the thumbnail**, when the BMW logo is used as a graphic element in Remotion, or when copy implies a relationship ("BMW diagnostics app", "official"). Say "works with BMW (standard OBD2)" only where true (Skanyx is a generic OBD2 reader; never imply BMW-specific coding or dealer functions; check CAPABILITY_GROUND_TRUTH.md). A caption line "Not affiliated with BMW AG" is cheap insurance when a make is central to a video. Naming other makes in text and hashtags for compatibility is the same referential use.
- Personal data in shot: our own plates are low-risk; **blur third-party plates and bystander faces** (GDPR); get written consent from team members who appear on camera or whose voice is cloned (see 7c).

## 9. Owner decisions

1. **Voice strategy (the decision everything else hangs on).** Options: (a) a team member records the voiceover; no AI label anywhere, every platform and the AI Act are out of the picture for the voice; (b) a designed ElevenLabs voice (Voice Design, owned by us, not a Default voice, which expire 2026-12-31); (c) an own-voice PVC of a team member, possibly dubbed into 13 languages. A hybrid is realistic: human voice for the 1-3 biggest languages, AI voice for the long tail. **Meta requires an AI label for (b) and (c) on a plain reading; the EU AI Act may require a visible "AI voice" mark (lawyer Q1/Q2).** The goal "viewers should not think AI made it" is achievable with (a); with (b)/(c) it conflicts with Meta's rule today and possibly with EU law.
2. **How much to label beyond the clear minimum.** Minimum defensible set if AI voice is used: Meta "AI label" on; TikTok "AI-generated" on; YouTube "AI use" = Yes (description label, YouTube says no reach effect). Optional, pending lawyer: a burned-in "AI voice" mark (Code of Practice style: "AI" acronym, start of video and repeated). Trade-off: TikTok's Nov 2025 AI-content slider may reduce reach for labelled posts; non-labelling risks removal, restriction and, under the AI Act, fines (SME cap = the lower of EUR 15 M or 3%).
3. **Whether to sign up to / follow the Code of Practice Section 2** as our labelling standard (voluntary; adherence is the recognised way to show Art 50(4) compliance). Following its icon/placement spec without signing is also possible.
4. **ElevenLabs plan.** Commercial use needs a paid plan, every published asset generated while subscribed and not on Beta features; PVC needs Creator+. **Eleven Music for a company needs Scale (<10 employees) or above (~USD 299/mo)**; otherwise use licensed stock music. Also: opt out of training in "Data use".
5. **Voice agreement** with any team member whose voice is cloned: scope, languages, term, revocation and its effect on published videos, pay, GDPR basis. (PVC lives on their account; they can stop sharing.)
6. **Demo data approach:** record the shipped Demo Mode, or fixtures replayed from a real capture of the filmed car, or invented fixtures with a "Demo data" note. Plus: "Sequences shortened" on sped-up app footage (Apple rule).
7. **Claims policy for scripts:** a named reviewer signs off each script against CAPABILITY_GROUND_TRUTH.md, REPAIR_PRICES.md and COMPETITOR_PRICES.md; "safe to drive" phrased as the app's per-code guidance, never as a verdict on the filmed car; "free" always qualified (adapter needed, Pro optional).
8. **BMW visibility rule:** incidental badges fine; no roundel thumbnails or logo graphics; "Not affiliated with BMW AG" line when a make is central.
9. **"Android" wording:** submit creatives for Android brand review as Google's guidelines ask, or keep the word "Android" out of on-screen graphics and rely on the Google Play badge plus "Google Play" text.
10. **Boosting organic posts** (TikTok Promote/Spark Ads, Meta boost, YouTube ads) turns them into ads and pulls in ad policies: TikTok reportedly requires AIGC disclosure labels in ads since 2026-07-21 (secondary sources), Meta shows AI info under "About this ad". Decide before the first boost.
